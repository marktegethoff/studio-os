Streak Shield Recovery Path.

An experiment frames a falsifiable claim, designs the minimum test that can falsify it, and forecasts what success and failure look like at short, medium, and long horizons using the drum simulation model.

2026-05-27Owner: Product / GrowthStatus: Planned

Hypothesis

IFWe show a streak shield — one free recovery per 7-day period — to users who break their first tracked streak in days 8–14, surfaced immediately at the moment of break with a clear recovery path
THEN30-day retention for that cohort increases by at least 12% relative to control
BECAUSEThe 61% abandonment cliff at days 8–14 is driven by perceived irrecoverable failure — users interpret a reset-to-zero as a definitive signal that they are not a "habit person," triggering exit rather than re-engagement; a bounded recovery path removes that interpretation without eliminating the cost of breaking habits
Falsifiable when30-day retention lifts ≥12% for the streak-shield cohort AND week-3 daily active rate is not lower than control — both conditions required; retention gain without maintained engagement proves the shield reduces urgency rather than rescues commitment

Experiment design

VariableValue
VariantOn first tracked streak break in days 8–14: streak counter holds, shield indicator appears ("1 recovery available this week"), user prompted to resume tomorrow — streak continues if next day completed
ControlCurrent behavior: streak counter resets to zero on missed day, no acknowledgment or recovery path shown
PopulationNew users (account age ≤ 14 days) who complete at least one tracked habit on day 7 or earlier and miss a tracked day for the first time in the days 8–14 window; excludes users who already churned before day 8 and users in any concurrent experiment touching the streak surface
Sample sizeMinimum 800 per arm (1,600 total) to detect a 12% retention lift at 80% power, α = 0.05; based on current new-user volume this requires two 30-day cohort intakes
Duration30 days per cohort (matching the primary retention metric horizon), two sequential cohort intakes — 60 days total data collection before analysis
AssignmentUser-level random assignment (50/50 split) at the moment the qualifying first streak break is detected; assignment persists for the full 30-day observation window

Drum simulation

Three time horizons. For each, name the expected behavior under success and the failure mode that would force a pivot.

Short-term (days 8–14)Success: Shield cohort same-day or next-day resume rate is meaningfully higher than control's post-reset return rate; abandonment in the days 8–14 window drops below 40% vs. current 61%.
Failure: Shield cohort abandons at the same rate as control — suggests the problem is genuine disengagement, not perceived irrecoverability; pause and investigate whether the shield UX was understood.
Medium-term (days 15–30)Success: Shield cohort maintains ≥ control week-3 daily active rate; streak lengths in the shield group at day 30 are equal to or longer than control streaks at day 30 — evidence that users who were retained remained genuinely engaged.
Failure: Shield cohort week-3 DAR drops below control — indicates the shield delayed exit without changing the underlying behavior pattern; the recovery path may have taught users that streaks are negotiable.
Long-term (months+)Success: Day-60 and day-90 retention tracks higher for the shield cohort; shield users accumulate longer net streaks over a 90-day window, supporting rollout of shield as a permanent mechanic.
Failure: Retained users plateau at week-5 or earlier at the same rate as control — shield postponed but did not prevent the same eventual disengagement, indicating a deeper onboarding or habit-formation gap the shield alone cannot address.

Success criteria

30-day retention rate for shield cohort ≥ control + 12 percentage points (primary win threshold)
Week-3 daily active rate for shield cohort ≥ control week-3 daily active rate (engagement integrity check — must not degrade)

Counter-metrics

What must NOT degrade. Guardrails that, if breached, end the experiment regardless of primary outcome.

Week-3 streak continuation rate in shield cohort must not fall more than 3 percentage points below control — a larger drop signals the shield is eroding the perceived cost of breaking habits, the core risk of the intervention
Shield usage rate must not exceed 60% of eligible cohort by week 4 — unusually high shield consumption would indicate users are deliberately using the recovery as a skipping mechanism rather than as an emergency buffer

Decision rule

If 30-day retention lifts ≥12% AND week-3 streak continuation rate is within 3 points of control AND shield usage rate stays below 60%: ship streak shield to all new users, schedule a day-90 follow-up to confirm the retention gain holds. If 30-day retention lifts 5–11% with no counter-metric breach: do not ship; run a follow-on experiment testing a more prominent recovery path or pairing the shield with a push notification before concluding the mechanic is worth the complexity. If 30-day retention is flat or negative, or any counter-metric breaches: roll back immediately, treat the problem as a disengagement issue rather than a perceived-failure issue, and investigate onboarding and habit-selection quality as the next lever.
