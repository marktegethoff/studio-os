Streak Shield Recovery Path

A

Hypothesis

IF · THEN · BECAUSE

If we offer one free streak recovery after a first break, then 30-day retention rises 12% over control, because a reset stops feeling final. 24/25 words

State the hypothesis in this form. If it does not fit the form, it is not ready to test.
B

Falsification condition

what would disprove it
  1. B1The experiment falsifies the hypothesis if 30-day retention lifts less than 12%, or if week-3 daily active rate falls below control.C1 · C2
  2. B2The experiment confirms the hypothesis if 30-day retention lifts at least 12% and week-3 daily active rate holds at control or above. Both conditions are required. A retention gain without held engagement shows that the shield lowers urgency. It does not rescue commitment.C1 · C2
  3. B3No concurrent experiment touches the streak surface. Assignment holds for the full 30-day window.invariant

D

Sample and duration

design · size · time
  1. D1Variant On a first tracked streak break in days 8–14, the streak counter holds. A shield indicator reads "1 recovery available this week". The user is prompted to resume tomorrow. The streak continues if the user completes the next day.
  2. D2Control Current behavior. The streak counter resets to zero on a missed day. The product shows no acknowledgment and no recovery path.
  3. D3Population Eligible users are new (account age ≤ 14 days). They complete a tracked habit by day 7. They then miss a tracked day for the first time in days 8–14. Excluded: users who churned before day 8, and users in any concurrent experiment on the streak surface.
  4. D4Assignment User-level random assignment, 50/50, when the product detects the qualifying first break. Assignment persists for the full 30-day observation window.
  5. D5
    Sample size per arm800 · min 800
    05001,000
    1,600 in total. This sample detects a 12% retention lift at 80% power and α = 0.05. Current new-user volume needs two 30-day cohort intakes.
  6. D6
    Duration per cohort30 days · min 30
    03060
    The 30 days match the horizon of the primary metric. Two sequential cohort intakes give 60 days of data before analysis.
Power the sample to detect the minimum effect of interest. Tie the duration to the natural cadence of the metric.
E

Decision rules

cites C1 · C3 · C4
  1. E1If 30-day retention lifts at least 12% and no counter-metric breaches, ship the shield to all new users. Schedule a day-90 follow-up to confirm that the gain holds.Ship
  2. E2If 30-day retention lifts 5–11% and no counter-metric breaches, do not ship. Run a follow-on experiment with a more prominent recovery path, or pair the shield with a push notification. Do this before you conclude that the mechanic is worth its complexity.Hold
  3. E3If 30-day retention is flat or negative, or any counter-metric breaches, roll back immediately. Treat the problem as disengagement, not perceived failure. Investigate onboarding and habit-selection quality as the next lever.Roll back
F

Risks

assumption register · binding first
  1. F1Binding assumption: perceived irrecoverable failure drives the 61% abandonment cliff in days 8–14. A reset to zero reads as proof that the user is not a "habit person", so the user exits and does not return. Mitigation: the short-term horizon G1 tests it. Equal abandonment in both arms falsifies it.binding
  2. F2The shield may delay exit and not prevent it. Mitigation: the week-3 condition in B2, and the day-60 and day-90 follow-up in G3.B2 · G3
  3. F3Users may use the shield to skip on purpose. Mitigation: counter-metric C4 caps shield usage at 60% of the eligible cohort by week 4.C4
The assumption-mapper names the binding assumption first. Each risk cites the item that tests it.
