A

Boundaries

trigger · two outcomes
A1 · TriggerA prospective client books a call. An email arrives, or a calendar invite is accepted. The architect now has a deadline and a specific audience. The need to send relevant work arises outside the product.
A2 · Successful outcome ✓The architect has a live link in the client's inbox and knows the client has opened it. They walk into the pitch sure that the right work is in front of the right person.
A3 · Failed outcome ✕The architect never sends anything. They abandon the flow and fall back to email attachments. Or they send a link they doubt looks right and cannot know whether the client opened it before the call.
A trigger is a real-world event, not a product action. A failed outcome is as specific as a successful one.

C

Entry and exit paths

how users arrive and leave
  1. C1Email or calendarA prospect confirms a call. The architect is in the inbox, not in Folio. They carry the client's name, project type, and a rough timeline. No Folio session is open.Entry
  2. C2Direct re-entryThe architect remembers a past share attempt mid-week and returns to finish or update it. They carry partial intent but have lost the context of the earlier session.Entry
  3. C3Post-editing impulseThe architect finishes updating a project and thinks "I should send this." This is the lowest-friction entry and the least intentional. Recency drives selection, not fit.Entry
  4. C4SuccessThe architect returns to email to prepare talking points. They carry the sent link and, if open tracking exists, confidence that the client engaged. They go into the pitch prepared.✓ Exit
  5. C5Failure or abandonmentThe architect exits Folio mid-selection and attaches PDFs in email. They carry a friction memory: "took too long, not worth it". That memory reinforces non-use.✕ Exit

D

Moments that matter

ranked · D1 first
  1. D1Trigger to first action: the gap between email and FolioThe impulse to share arises in a different context than the tool that enables it. The 77% of users who do not adopt share are not failing at the feature. They never reach it. The trigger is mislocated: Folio waits for users to arrive instead of meeting them when the need fires. A share-flow fix that skips this gap leaves the adoption ceiling in place.cites B1
  2. D2Share: sending without seeingArchitects do not send work they doubt. The confidence gap at link generation is the proximate cause of abandonment for users who do reach the feature. It has three parts: no client-facing preview, no access transparency, and no sense of what the recipient will see. A user who sees what the client sees will send. One who cannot will hesitate, second-guess, and often revert to attachments.cites B4
